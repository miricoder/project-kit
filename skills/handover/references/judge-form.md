# The judge form

What every judge is asked, what a verdict must contain, and how three verdicts become one
ruling. The brief each judge receives is generated from `scripts/form.py`, and so is the
validator, so the two cannot drift. A test fails if this page and `form.py` disagree.

## What a judge reads

`judge/round-<n>/brief.md`, generated by `pk judge case`. It contains, in order:

1. The rules of the review: judge only the case, cite evidence, rate every criterion,
   reply with one JSON object.
2. The verdicts and criteria below.
3. The reply format, with this round's `case_id` filled in.
4. From round 2 on: the previous ruling's open fixes and questions, and `docs/changes.md`,
   the author's account of what changed, fix by fix.
5. The author's strongest counter-argument, from the last section of `docs/spec.md`.
6. The whole case: `docs/research.md`, `docs/proposal.md`, `docs/spec.md`,
   `docs/acceptance.feature`.

`case_id` is a hash of everything above. A verdict carrying a different one judged a
different version of the plan and is refused.

## Verdicts

Least to most severe. The order is the combination rule.

| Verdict | Meaning | Must include |
|---|---|---|
| `SOUND` | Buildable as written. | no `blocker` finding, no `fail` rating |
| `FIX FIRST` | Right direction; specific blocking problems first. | at least one `blocker`, each with a `fix` |
| `NEEDS INFO` | The case lacks something needed to judge it. | at least one question |
| `SPLIT` | More than one product, or too large for one plan. | at least one finding saying where to cut |
| `REJECT` | Fundamentally unsound. | at least one finding saying why |

## Criteria

Every criterion is rated exactly once: `pass`, `concern` or `fail`. A `concern` or `fail`
carries a note.

| id | Question |
|---|---|
| `job_fit` | Does v1 do the stated job end to end for the named person? |
| `scope` | Is the v1 cut the smallest thing that does the job, and does the spec stay inside it and its out-of-scope list? |
| `feasibility` | Can the core capability work as designed? Are the technical risks named and credible? |
| `failure_modes` | Is the failure-mode table complete, and does every row have an acceptance scenario? |
| `data_and_rollback` | Is the data model sound, are migrations versioned, and can both code and data be rolled back? |
| `security_privacy` | Is sensitive data identified and protected, are secrets kept out of code and logs? |
| `testability` | Are the acceptance criteria executable, and do they cover every v1 capability? |
| `cost` | Are the effort and running-cost estimates credible, and is the biggest uncertainty addressed? |
| `counter_argument` | Does the design survive its own strongest counter-argument? |

## Findings

| Field | Values |
|---|---|
| `criterion` | one of the criteria ids |
| `severity` | `blocker` (must change before building), `major` (should change), `minor` (worth noting) |
| `confidence` | `CITED` (points at specific text), `REASONED` (follows from it), `SPECULATING` (plausible, neither confirmed nor ruled out) |
| `claim` | what is wrong |
| `evidence` | the document and section it rests on |
| `fix` | the concrete change; required for `blocker` and `major` |

## Reply format

```json
{
  "form_version": 1,
  "case_id": "<copied from the brief>",
  "verdict": "FIX FIRST",
  "summary": "Two or three sentences: the verdict and the main reason.",
  "criteria": [
    {"id": "job_fit", "rating": "pass", "note": ""},
    {"id": "data_and_rollback", "rating": "fail", "note": "No reverse migration."}
  ],
  "findings": [
    {"criterion": "data_and_rollback", "severity": "blocker", "confidence": "CITED",
     "claim": "The migration is one-way.", "evidence": "spec.md, Rollback",
     "fix": "Add a reverse migration and a scenario that exercises it."}
  ],
  "questions": []
}
```

(Abbreviated: a real reply rates all nine criteria.)

## What is checked before a verdict counts

A reply that fails any of these is stored but not counted; `pk judge status` lists why.

- It parses as exactly one JSON object (a code fence around it is fine).
- `form_version` and `case_id` match this round.
- Every criterion is rated exactly once with a valid rating; concerns and failures have notes.
- Every finding names a known criterion, severity and confidence, has a claim and evidence,
  and blockers and majors have a fix.
- The verdict agrees with the findings, as in the verdict table.
- **Who sent it is recorded by the runner, never read from the reply.** A judge cannot claim
  to be another family.

## The panel

Exactly three seats, three distinct families, none of them `anthropic`: Claude wrote the
plan, and a Claude judge would share its priors. Each seat is reached one of three ways:

| Transport | When | Spend |
|---|---|---|
| `api_key` | a key for that family in `~/.claude/project-kit/.env` | estimated from token usage, added to the budget |
| `cli` | a logged-in subscription CLI (`codex` for openai) | none |
| `paste` | `PROJECT_KIT_PASTE_SEATS=google,moonshot` in the `.env`, or `--paste <family>` | none; you paste `brief.md` and save the reply |

## How three verdicts become a ruling

1. **The median by severity.** The ruling is the most severe verdict that at least two of
   the three judges reached or exceeded. `SOUND, SOUND, REJECT` is `SOUND`;
   `SOUND, FIX FIRST, REJECT` is `FIX FIRST`. One judge alone can neither sink a plan nor
   pass it; the outlier is reported as dissent.
2. **Open fixes** are every `blocker` finding from any judge, unless the ruling is `SOUND`,
   in which case a minority's blockers are listed separately and do not hold the gate.
   They are ranked: severity, then confidence (`CITED` first), then criterion order.
3. **Tier.** `standard` (the default) applies the rule as is. At `high`, a `FIX FIRST`
   whose blockers are all `SPECULATING` becomes `NEEDS INFO`. Use `standard` for greenfield
   plans: nothing is runnable yet, so every finding is speculative by necessity.

## The gate

The judge gate before `/initiate` passes on a recorded `SOUND`, or a `FIX FIRST` with no fix
left open. `REJECT`, `SPLIT` and `NEEDS INFO` never pass. Open fixes close only by revising
the plan and judging it again; there is no command that closes one by assertion.

`pk judge record` re-derives the ruling from the raw replies and rebuilds the case from
`docs/` before recording. A hand-edited `ruling.json`, or a plan edited after it was judged,
is refused.

At most three rounds are judged before the user must agree to another
(`--allow-extra-round`).
